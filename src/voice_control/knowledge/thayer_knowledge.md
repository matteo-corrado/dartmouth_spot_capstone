- **Original name:** Thayer School of Civil Engineering. The school dropped "Civil" in 1941 when it added electrical and mechanical engineering courses.

- **First class size:** Three students.
- **Part of:** Dartmouth College, an Ivy League university.

- **Mailing address:** 14 Engineering Drive, Hanover, NH 03755.
- **Main buildings:** Cummings Hall (1939), MacLean Engineering Sciences Center (2006), and the Class of 1982 Engineering and Computer Science Center (ECSC, 2022).

- **Distinctive trait:** one of the oldest professional engineering schools in the United States.
- **Distinctive trait:** organized as a single, unified school of engineering rather than separate departments.

## History

### The founding story

Sylvanus Thayer (1785–1872) graduated from Dartmouth in 1807 as valedictorian. He went straight to West Point and later became its fifth superintendent, from 1817 to 1833. His sweeping reforms there earned him the nickname "Father of West Point."

After thirty years of service in the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Thayer wanted to give his alma mater the kind of rigorous engineering education he had built at West Point — but for civilians. In 1867, at age 81, he donated funds to Dartmouth's trustees to create a school of engineering. The gift eventually grew to about $70,000, and he added his personal library of engineering books, manuscripts, and plates.

Thayer believed engineers needed a broad foundation in the arts and sciences to be ready for "the most responsible positions and the most difficult service." That principle still shapes how Dartmouth Engineering is taught today. Sylvanus Thayer died in 1872, just one year after the school began holding classes.

### Opening day

Initially called the **Thayer School of Civil Engineering**, the school opened in 1871 with **three students** and five small rooms borrowed in Wentworth, Reed, and Thornton Halls on the Dartmouth campus. Thayer would not have a building of its own for nearly seventy years.

### Robert Fletcher, the first dean

After a three-year search, 23-year-old Lieutenant **Robert Fletcher** was named the school's first dean and its only faculty member. He served as dean for 47 years, then continued teaching for another 18 years. He is sometimes called the "father of Thayer" for the role he played in shaping it.

Under Fletcher, the early curriculum had 12 courses including Surveying, Mechanics, Resistance of Materials, Bridges and Roofs, Hydraulic Works, Heat-Engines, Sanitary Engineering, and Masonry and Foundations.

### Key milestones

- **1867** — Sylvanus Thayer endows the school.
- **1871** — First classes begin with three students in borrowed rooms.
- **1939** — Cummings Memorial Hall opens, the first building constructed specifically for Thayer.
- **1941** — The school officially drops "Civil" from its name and adds electrical and mechanical engineering courses, becoming Thayer School of Engineering.
- **1945–1961** — Dean William P. Kimball establishes Thayer's first master's-degree programs.
- **1947** — Two wings are added to Cummings Hall, one for electrical and one for mechanical engineering.
- **1961** — Dean Myron Tribus introduces an integrated engineering curriculum and starts Thayer's first doctoral program.
- **1964** — Thayer awards its first PhD, to Michael Turner.
- **1978** — The Cook Engineering Design Center is founded to bridge industry and the school.
- **1987** — Cummings Hall expands with the addition of the Great Hall, doubling Thayer's size.
- **1995–1998** — Elsa Garmire serves as Thayer's first woman dean.
- **2006** — MacLean Engineering Sciences Center opens, doubling Thayer's facilities again.
- **2008** — Dartmouth Engineering launches the nation's first PhD Innovation Program.
- **2016** — Dartmouth becomes the first national research university to graduate a class of engineers that is mostly women — 54% of AB engineering degrees go to women.
- **2019** — Groundbreaking for the new Engineering and Computer Science Center.
- **2022** — The Class of 1982 Engineering and Computer Science Center opens — the largest construction project in Dartmouth's history. Construction was completed in March 2022 and the building was formally dedicated on May 13, 2022.

## Academic programs

Dartmouth Engineering is unusual: it does not have separate departments. Students and faculty work across disciplines in a single integrated school.

### Undergraduate

- **AB in Engineering Sciences** — A liberal-arts bachelor of arts in engineering, completed as part of Dartmouth's broader undergraduate curriculum.
- **BE (Bachelor of Engineering)** — A professional fifth-year degree built on top of the AB. The BE is accredited by the Engineering Accreditation Commission of ABET, which qualifies graduates for professional engineering licensure.

Most Dartmouth undergrads start with the AB and add courses to earn the professional BE.

### Graduate

- **MEM — Master of Engineering Management** — Combines graduate-level engineering coursework with management classes from Dartmouth's Tuck School of Business. Dartmouth's MEM is a founding member of the Master of Engineering Management Programs Consortium.
- **MEng — Master of Engineering** — Emphasizes applied research, mathematics, and depth-with-breadth across engineering disciplines.
- **MS — Master of Science** — A research-focused master's degree.
- **PhD in Engineering Sciences** — Flexible across research areas; prepares students for academic or professional careers.
- **PhD Innovation Program (PhD-I)** — Launched in 2008, the nation's first doctoral program built around translating research into commercial impact. PhD-I students complete normal PhD requirements plus extra coursework in business, innovation, and entrepreneurship, and spend up to six months in an industry internship.

### Dual and joint degrees

Thayer offers MD/PhD and other joint degrees with the Geisel School of Medicine, plus combined options with Dartmouth's other graduate schools.

## Faculty and research

### Three big research themes

Thayer organizes its research around three pillars:

1. **Engineering in Medicine**
2. **Energy Technologies**
3. **Complex Systems**

Within those, faculty work across biological and chemical engineering, biomedical engineering, electrical and computer engineering, energy engineering, materials science, and mechanical, operations, and systems engineering — all under one roof.

### A few flagship research areas

These are evergreen — they've been Thayer strengths for many years.

- **Optics and biomedical imaging.** Thayer's Optics in Medicine Lab develops light-based tools for cancer diagnosis, image-guided surgery, and radiotherapy. It's one of the leading academic groups in image-guided medicine.
- **Cold-region engineering and ice mechanics.** Thayer has a long tradition of studying ice and frozen ground. The Ice Research Lab studies the mechanics of ice at very small and very large scales, and a separate group studies how ice sheets shape sea level. Thayer also works closely with the U.S. Army Cold Regions Research and Engineering Laboratory (CRREL), which sits right next to campus.
- **Orthopaedic biomechanics.** The Dartmouth Biomedical Engineering Center for Orthopaedics, in Cummings Hall, runs one of the world's largest collections of artificial joints retrieved from real patients — over 23,000 of them — to study how implants wear out inside the body.
- **Cellulosic biofuels.** Lee Lynd's lab is a world center for turning plant material into renewable fuel.
- **Image sensors.** Eric Fossum, a Thayer professor, invented the CMOS image sensor — the technology in nearly every smartphone camera today. He won the Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering in 2017 and the U.S. National Medal of Technology and Innovation in 2025.
- **Energy systems and the grid.** Multiple labs study how to make the electricity grid resilient and how to model the energy transition.
- **Robotics and autonomy.** Faculty work on dynamic systems, controls, and multi-agent autonomy.
- **Photonics and integrated electronics.** Several labs build miniature optical devices, integrated photonic systems, and nanomaterial electronics.

Thayer publishes a directory of about fifty named research labs at engineering.dartmouth.edu/research/labs. The labs are spread across all three buildings.

### Research culture

- About **86 core faculty**, in a community of around **140** total researchers and instructors.
- Over **50%** of tenure-track faculty have founded startups.
- Over **65%** of tenure-track faculty hold patents.
- **About 46%** of Thayer's undergraduate engineering class identifies as female — well above the national average for engineering schools.
- Annual research expenditures: about **$37 million**.

## Buildings and spaces

Thayer's classrooms, labs, and offices live in three connected buildings on the west end of Dartmouth's campus, in what's called the **West End District**. The three buildings are physically connected — you can walk from one to the next without going outside.

### Cummings Hall

- Full name: **Horace Cummings Memorial Hall**.
- **Opened in 1939** — the first building constructed specifically for Thayer School. For nearly 70 years before Cummings, Thayer had to borrow rooms in other Dartmouth buildings.
- Named in memory of Horace Cummings, Dartmouth Class of 1862, husband of donor Jeannette Cummings.
- **1947:** Two wings added — one for electrical engineering, one for mechanical engineering.
- **1987:** Expanded with the addition of the Great Hall, doubling Thayer's size.
- Houses the **machine shop** (the "MShop") in its basement, the **CAD Lab**, the Electronics Project Lab, fluids and biotech instructional labs, the rapid-prototyping and 3D-printing room, the microengineering lab, and the Dartmouth Biomedical Engineering Center for Orthopaedics.

### MacLean Engineering Sciences Center

- Often called **MacLean ESC**.
- **Opened in 2006**, doubling Thayer's facilities at the time.
- Named for **Barry MacLean** (Dartmouth Class of 1960, Thayer 1961) and his wife **Mary Ann MacLean**. Barry MacLean is a longtime Thayer supporter and trustee — described in Thayer's own materials as the single largest donor in the school's history.
- Houses the visitor reception desk, the **Couch Project Lab**, the **Allyn Large-Frame Project Workshop** (home base for the Dartmouth Formula Racing team), the Fahey Advanced Design Lab, materials processing and mechanical testing labs, the microscopy lab, X-ray diffraction, ice research labs, classrooms, and faculty offices.

### Class of 1982 Engineering and Computer Science Center (ECSC)

- The newest building, opened in 2022.
- Construction completed in March 2022 and formally dedicated on **May 13, 2022**, during the Dartmouth Innovation and Technology Festival.
- About **160,000 square feet** across four above-ground levels, plus a three-level underground parking garage (the Emily and Errik Anderson Parking Garage) with 340 spaces.
- Named in honor of the **Dartmouth Class of 1982**, whose collective gifts toward the building exceeded $50 million.
- **The largest construction project in Dartmouth's history**, funded entirely from philanthropy.
- Houses Thayer engineering faculty and labs, **the entire Dartmouth Department of Computer Science**, the **Magnuson Center for Entrepreneurship**, the **DALI Lab** (Digital Applied Learning and Innovation), the **Harold Edward Cable Makerspace**, the **Stuart Family Design Commons**, the Electron Microscopy Suite, two team-based learning classrooms, and a café called "Back of the Napkin."
- Connects to MacLean ESC through the sunlit **Opus Foundation Bridge** and an underground tunnel.

### The Machine Shop ("MShop")

- Located in the **basement of Cummings Hall**.
- A teaching workshop where students learn problem-solving and teamwork through hands-on building.
- Equipment includes saws, mills, lathes, welding stations, woodworking tools, and 3D printers.
- Many engineering classes use the MShop, including ENGS 21, the introductory project course, and ENGS 33 (Solid Mechanics).

### Other notable spaces

- **DALI Lab (Digital Applied Learning and Innovation)** — a project lab in the ECSC where students collaborate with real partners — companies, nonprofits, researchers — to design and build mobile apps, websites, virtual and augmented reality experiences, and digital art.
- **Harold Edward Cable Makerspace** — a personal-projects makerspace in the ECSC, distinct from the class-focused MShop. It's where students go to build things for clubs, fun, or experiments of their own.
- **Electron Microscopy Suite** — shared imaging facility in the ECSC.
- **WeFab** — a shared microfabrication cleanroom.

## Student life and culture

### Hands-on, project-based engineering

Thayer is famous for putting students into real engineering problems early. From their first year, undergraduates work in teams to design, build, test, and iterate prototypes.

- **ENGS 21 (Introduction to Engineering)** — A team-based course where students develop solutions to real problems, build prototypes, research patents, draft a business plan, and present to a review board. Students get trained on machine-shop tools as part of the course.
- **ENGS 89/90** — The two-term BE capstone sequence. Students work in industry-sponsored teams through the Cook Engineering Design Center to deliver real engineering solutions, from mid-September through early March.
- **ENGS 15 — Senior Design Challenge** — A two-term capstone option where students from across Dartmouth (not just engineering majors) work in interdisciplinary teams on human-centered design projects with Upper Valley community partners.
- **LEAP (Learn Engineering through Applied Projects)** — A first-year program that pairs students with non-profit organizations on environmental and social-impact projects.

### Famous student projects

Through the Cook Engineering Design Center, students have built things like:

- Low-sensory pods for special-needs children at the Children's Hospital at Dartmouth-Hitchcock.
- A concept greenhouse on Mars for the NASA Big Ideas Challenge.
- The **Mobile Virtual Player**, a remote-controlled tackling dummy designed to help reduce concussions in football.

### Engineering clubs and student groups

- **DFR — Dartmouth Formula Racing** — designs and builds a Formula-style race car each year and competes against other universities.
- **DHE — Dartmouth Humanitarian Engineering** — engineering projects that help communities around the world.
- **SWE — Society of Women Engineers** — supports women in engineering with mentorship, events, and community.
- **Dartmouth Aerospace Engineering Club** — for students interested in rockets, satellites, and flight.

### Undergraduate research

Thayer's faculty welcome undergraduates into real research projects.

- **FYREE — First Year Research in Engineering Experience** — lets first-year students join research labs early.
- Many undergraduates end up contributing to real projects in real labs across the school.

### Gender parity

In 2016, Dartmouth became the first national research university to award a majority of its undergraduate engineering degrees to women, with women earning **54%** of the AB engineering degrees that year. Thayer has stayed well ahead of the national average ever since.

### Entrepreneurship culture

The school takes pride in how many faculty and students start companies. Over half of tenure-track faculty have founded startups, and the PhD Innovation Program is designed specifically around launching ventures from PhD research.

## Dartmouth College — the bigger picture

Thayer is one of Dartmouth's professional schools. Visitors often want to know more about Dartmouth as a whole, so here are evergreen facts.

### Founding

- **Founded:** December 13, 1769.
- **Founder:** Eleazar Wheelock, a Yale graduate and Congregational minister from Connecticut.
- **Charter:** Granted by King George III through New Hampshire's royal governor John Wentworth, "for the education and instruction of youth of the Indian tribes... and also of English youth and any others."
- **Named for:** William Legge, 2nd Earl of Dartmouth, an English nobleman who supported Wheelock's work.
- **Place in history:** Dartmouth is the **ninth-oldest college in the United States** and the **last of the nine "colonial colleges"** chartered before the American Revolution. It was founded seven years before the Declaration of Independence.

### The Dartmouth College Case (1819)

In 1816, New Hampshire tried to take over Dartmouth and convert it into a public university. Dartmouth fought back, and **Daniel Webster** — Dartmouth Class of 1801 — argued the case before the U.S. Supreme Court in 1819. The Marshall Court ruled in Dartmouth's favor, finding that the state's takeover was an illegal impairment of Dartmouth's charter. Webster's famous closing line: *"It is, sir, as I have said, a small college. And yet there are those who love it."* The case is a landmark in American constitutional law on contracts and corporate charters.

### The other schools at Dartmouth

- **Geisel School of Medicine** — Founded 1797, the **fourth-oldest medical school** in the United States. Renamed in 2012 for Audrey and Theodor "Dr. Seuss" Geisel.
- **Tuck School of Business** — Founded 1900, the **world's first institution to offer a master's degree in business administration**.
- **Thayer School of Engineering** — Founded 1867 by Sylvanus Thayer.
- **Frank J. Guarini School of Graduate and Advanced Studies** — Founded 2016 (named 2018), Dartmouth's first new school in more than 150 years.

### The D-Plan

Dartmouth runs **year-round on four ten-week academic terms**: fall, winter, spring, and summer. Students must be in residence for fall, winter, and spring of freshman year, two terms of senior year, and one summer (usually after sophomore year — the famous "Sophomore Summer"). The D-Plan was instituted in the early 1970s when Dartmouth went co-educational and ran out of dorm beds. It means classes are in session at Dartmouth in the summer, which is unusual — the campus is alive year-round.

### Dartmouth and computing

Dartmouth has a special place in computing history.

- In **1964**, professors **John G. Kemeny** and **Thomas E. Kurtz** designed the **BASIC** programming language at Dartmouth. BASIC was created so that any student — not just engineers and mathematicians — could learn to write computer programs. The first BASIC program ran at Dartmouth on May 1, 1964, at 4 a.m.
- BASIC ran on the **Dartmouth Time-Sharing System**, one of the first general-purpose interactive time-sharing systems in the world. By 1968, most Dartmouth students and faculty were using it.
- In the summer of **1956**, a group of researchers organized the **Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence**, which is widely considered the founding event of AI as a field. Dartmouth professor John McCarthy coined the term **"artificial intelligence"** in the proposal for that workshop.
- Kemeny later became Dartmouth's president, from 1970 to 1981.

### Traditions

- **Dartmouth Green** color, officially adopted in **1866**.
- **"The Big Green"** — the athletic nickname. Dartmouth has **no official animal mascot**.
- **The Lone Pine** — Dartmouth's longest-standing visual symbol. It refers to a real pine tree that stood on a hill near campus from the late 1700s. By 1828 it was a senior gathering spot. The original tree was damaged by storms and cut down in 1895, but the Lone Pine still appears in Dartmouth's branding today.
- **Dartmouth Outing Club (DOC)** — Founded December 14, 1909. The DOC pioneered organized college skiing in America and still maintains over seventy miles of the Appalachian Trail.
- **Winter Carnival** — Held since 1910 (named "Winter Carnival" in 1911). Founded by the DOC. Famous for skiing, snow sculptures, and a giant snow sculpture on the campus Green.
- **Homecoming and the Bonfire** — Established in 1895 by President William Jewett Tucker. The freshman class traditionally runs around an enormous homecoming bonfire on the Green.
- **First-Year Trips** — Since 1935. More than 90% of incoming Dartmouth students do a four-day outdoor trip with their classmates that ends at the Moosilauke Ravine Lodge in the White Mountains.
- **Commencement** — Dartmouth's commencement has been held continuously since 1771, making it one of the oldest unbroken commencement traditions in the U.S. The Class of 1879 Trumpeters open the ceremonies with fanfares from Baker Tower.

### Notable Dartmouth places

- **The Green** — Dartmouth's central five-acre lawn, cleared in 1771, the literal and symbolic heart of campus.
- **Dartmouth Hall** — the iconic white Georgian-style building that anchors the visual image of Dartmouth.
- **Baker-Berry Library** — Dartmouth's main library. Baker Memorial Library opened in 1928; Berry Library was added in 2002. Baker Tower's spire is one of Dartmouth's most recognizable features. Baker Library houses the José Clemente Orozco mural cycle *The Epic of American Civilization*.
- **Hopkins Center for the Arts ("the Hop")** — Opened in 1962, designed by Wallace Harrison, who later designed the matching façade of the Metropolitan Opera House at Lincoln Center.
- **Hood Museum of Art** — Dartmouth's art museum, adjacent to the Hopkins Center.

## Practical visitor information

### Where Dartmouth is

- **Hanover, New Hampshire**, in the rural Upper Valley region.
- The campus sits on a terrace above the **Connecticut River**, which forms the border between New Hampshire and Vermont.
- The main campus is about **269 acres**.

### Getting to Hanover

- Hanover is a small town with no major airport. Visitors typically arrive by car.
- The **Dartmouth Coach** is a long-running shuttle bus service that runs from Boston Logan Airport, Boston South Station, and New York City directly to Hanover.

### When campus is in session

- Because of the D-Plan, classes run all four seasons — including summer. There is rarely a fully empty time on campus.

### Visiting

- **Student-led tours** of Dartmouth College are offered through the admissions office.
- Visitors are usually welcome to walk into Thayer's main lobbies during business hours and check in at the reception desk in MacLean.
- The **Hood Museum of Art**, **Hopkins Center for the Arts**, and **Baker-Berry Library** (with the Orozco mural) are all open to the public.
- The **Hanover Inn**, on the corner of the Green, is the on-campus hotel run by Dartmouth.

### Sitting in on classes

Specific class-visit policies change. The friendly answer is: ask at the front desk in MacLean, or email Thayer's office of student affairs.

## How Thayer is different from other engineering schools

If a visitor asks how Thayer compares to bigger engineering schools, here are evergreen distinctions:

- **It's small.** Thayer enrolls only a few hundred undergraduates and a few hundred graduate students — much smaller than typical engineering schools.
- **No departments.** All engineering disciplines are combined into one school, which makes it easier for students and professors from different specialties to work together.
- **Liberal-arts roots.** Most undergrads start with the AB in engineering sciences as part of Dartmouth's liberal-arts curriculum, and add the professional BE on top. The idea, going back to Sylvanus Thayer himself, is that engineers should be broadly educated.
- **Hands-on from day one.** Students design and build real projects from their very first engineering course.
- **Industry-connected capstones.** Through the Cook Engineering Design Center, undergraduate teams do a year-long real project for a real company.
- **Innovation-friendly PhD.** Thayer launched the nation's first PhD Innovation Program in 2008, training doctoral students to turn their research into companies.

## Notes on uncertainty and source contradictions

- **Cummings Hall opening year.** Thayer's official history page says **1939**. Some sources (including Wikipedia) say 1946. The 1939 date is correct: it's confirmed by Thayer's own timeline, by the school's 150th-anniversary history, and by a contemporary 1945 Dartmouth Alumni Magazine article. This pack uses **1939**.
- **Cummings Hall expansion year.** The Great Hall expansion was in **1987**, not 1989 (Wikipedia is wrong here too). The 1947 wings are a separate, earlier expansion.
- **Founding date wording.** Thayer was endowed in **1867** and held its first classes in **1871**. Both years are technically correct as a "founding year" — this pack uses 1867 as the founding year and notes 1871 as the year of first classes.
- **First woman dean.** Elsa Garmire (1995–1998) was Thayer's first woman dean. Alexis Abramson (2019–2024) was the second.
- **Current dean.** As of early 2026, Doug Van Citters is interim dean. A search is in progress for a permanent replacement. If you're asked who the current dean is and significant time has passed, suggest the visitor check the Thayer website.
- **Number of total students.** Sources give different counts depending on the year. The current number is around 740 across all programs. If a visitor needs an exact count, suggest they ask at the front desk.
- **Famous alumni.** This pack intentionally omits names that couldn't be confirmed in authoritative sources. Spot should never invent a "famous alum" when asked.
- **Faculty rosters change.** Specific lab membership and current course assignments are deliberately not in this pack.
